## 3. CF alignment

Ethan reported from the CF governance panel (met about two weeks earlier): no definitive decision. From the Unidata/NetCDF side, Dave Blodgett's "NZ" work will be treated as the next generation of NCZarr (which is limited to Zarr v2), ideally developed in public, with the C and Java NetCDF libraries implementing whatever comes out. That leaves two paths standing: an NZ-style mapping of existing CF into Zarr, and Patrick's more extensive rethink; the governance panel will not stop anyone pursuing either. How either path maps onto GeoZarr (extension versus convention) remains open.

Patrick's argument: NCZarr is a straight copy of the traditional NetCDF/HDF5 encoding into Zarr, not aligned with GeoZarr's conventions. CF is roughly 30 years old and rests on decisions taken for NetCDF 3; this is the moment to revisit them. The commitment should be backwards compatibility with CF *semantics* rather than with the encoding, concretely meaning a preferably lossless translation from a CF NetCDF dataset to a Zarr-native encoding; his convention follows OGC terminology and structure. Chris added historical context (the spherical-Earth assumptions of 100 to 200 km models, the two-year leap-second argument) and endorsed semantic compatibility via controlled vocabularies, while warning compromise will be tough; Patrick corrected one point for the record: leap seconds belong to UTC, not the Gregorian calendar, and CF now has a defensible UTC calendar definition.

**Decided:** Patrick will share his CF-aligned convention on GitHub before the next meeting (he is not on Agora, being a non-member).

## 4. Observational semantics and I-ADOPT

Kathi laid out her semantics case: CF standard names micro-encode and aggregate concepts (her example: the concentration of lead in the liver of a fish in a river) that the I-ADOPT ontology deliberately disaggregates into resolvable concepts (property, object of interest, matrix, context, constraints); for anyone outside the CF world, the standard-names list is a closed shop. She is looking for where in the GeoZarr model richer, OMS-style semantics can plug in, beyond a single standard-name string. Ethan noted there are now roughly 5,000 to 6,000 standard names, that CF people are already involved in the I-ADOPT mapping work (including the workshops Kathi co-ran with Barbara Magagna in Vienna), and that the standard-names experts (Alison Pamment and others) are the ones to involve, since neither he nor Patrick are standard-names people.

**Decided:** rather than solving it in a meeting, Kathi will write a one-to-two-page proposal for the next meeting so the group can review it properly.

## 5. V1 readiness

GDAL carries the most prominent implementation support, arriving in GDAL 3.11, scheduled for release on 4 May, the day before the next meeting. **Decided:** keep the May target for the V1 release candidate and reassess on 6 May, with the understanding that CF alignment will land in a subsequent GeoZarr release rather than 1.0.

Multiscales read tile size: OpenLayers implemented read support but had to parse the shard codec to discover read chunk sizes. Max has a suggestion plus proof-of-concepts in OpenLayers and zarr-layer for carrying read tile size in the Multiscales convention, and will open the PRs that week, shared via GitHub and Agora.

## 6. Governance

The co-chair invitation stands; Max will repeat it every meeting until an OGC member steps up. Chris gave a primer for the voting stage ahead: identify the charter members (voting from the group's start), distinguish voting members from observers (observers typically attend about three meetings first), and set quorum deliberately (roughly a third of voting members elsewhere in OGC, perhaps a quarter here), since making everyone a voting member and then deciding with two attendees is no consensus. He also noted OGC's technical committee policies are being rewritten and simplified over the coming months, and that Agora gained Zoho-backed file stores that day, with chairs holding edit rights. Kathi added that proxy voting ("floating proxy tokens" as practiced in OMS) keeps decisions moving with small turnouts.

## 7. Helsinki (OGC member meeting, June)

Kathi is organizing a cross-SWG effort continuing the Delft gathering of a few years ago: getting every group that puts data on spatial structures into one room, so the description of observational data stops being reinvented per group. OMS semantics went into CovJSON successfully; TimeseriesML needs Zarr among its encodings, with momentum from WMO (WaterML timing) and FAO soil work, and OGC's Geospatial Observation Needs And Requirements (GONAR) SWG heading in the same direction. Chris and Mark Burgoyne (UK Met Office) will attend; session request forms have been posted by Scott Simmons, and a linked one-day track of the related SWGs was floated in place of isolated sessions. Kathi will email Max the list of groups and contacts so he can argue for travel funding; Max will request a GeoZarr session.

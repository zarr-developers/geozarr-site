# GeoZarr SWG Monthly Meeting, 3 December 2025

**Date:** Wednesday 3 December 2025
**Attendees named in the transcript:** Christophe Noël (chair), Max Jones, Ryan Abernathey, Emmanuel Mathot, Tyler Erickson, Patrick Van Laake, Guilherme Castelão
**Subject of the meeting:** SWG chairs, contributing guide, specification simplification, and the Zarr Conventions Framework

*Revised in September 2026 against the meeting transcript.*

## 1. Chairs

The current chair mandate, a two-year period, ends in February; a call for nominations opens in January with the election in February. Brianna Pagán stepped down from her chair role some weeks earlier, and Max Jones was the sole candidate for the resulting interim co-chair position. With no objections raised, Max was approved as interim co-chair, and Christophe will notify OGC by email. Christophe announced that he will also step down at the end of the mandate, so a second chair to support Max is sought; volunteers for the February election should contact Christophe or any member.

## 2. Contributing guide and PR review process

Christophe committed a basic draft contributing guide (objectives, scope of change, participation, maintenance structure, versioning, release, roadmap) and invited pull requests to refactor it. Max flagged that the draft's recommendation to review PRs during monthly SWG meetings would slow all changes to a monthly cadence. Ryan proposed working asynchronously and reserving the meetings for topics without consensus, which Christophe supported.

**Decided:** PRs are reviewed and merged asynchronously on GitHub; meetings focus on unresolved questions.

## 3. Introductions

Patrick Van Laake joined for the first time: he works in R, is deeply involved with CF (author of an R package providing a CF layer on top of NetCDF, and of a fairly complete Zarr v3 R package), and has worked with the ISO 19100 series and OGC drafting since their beginnings. He noted the CF community is starting to look at taking CF beyond the NetCDF format, which motivates his interest in GeoZarr becoming an OGC standard. At the close of the meeting, Guilherme (Gui) Castelão (NREL) also introduced himself: an oceanography background, working on data standards for gliders and Argo from the firmware level up, joining to learn and contribute.

## 4. Simplifying the specification

Christophe recounted that the structure adopted around mid-2025, a unified data model with encodings defined against it, had become complex and poorly understood. He opened a PR simplifying the section that presents the abstract data model, with a summary page of his current understanding of GeoZarr's role, and asked for feedback. He explained why the CDM keeps coming up: it defines just groups, attributes, variables, and dimensions, so anything that maps to it (CF attributes, GeoTIFF metadata) maps easily onto Zarr; the gaps are the features Zarr lacks (named or shared dimensions, some NetCDF data types), and indeed his original motivation was the absence of named dimensions in Zarr v2.

Emmanuel argued the group is over-conceptualizing: the developers he points at GeoZarr ask very simple questions (where do I put my CRS, my projection, my overviews), and the Zarr conventions work now provides a solid basis to include directly. Patrick seconded this: rather than translating CF into Zarr wholesale (already done by UCAR as NCZarr, and in his view not the way to go), CF should be disassembled into profiles or extensions on top of a basic spatial foundation, since not everybody needs parametric vertical coordinates or discrete sampling geometries. Ryan proposed resolving the long-standing scope tension with an incremental roadmap: release the most urgent pieces first (CRS, then overviews, then vertical coordinates and so on), keeping the bigger ambitions such as a common data model open as later steps rather than prerequisites.

## 5. Zarr Conventions Framework

Max presented the Zarr Conventions Framework, an outcome of the October Zarr Summit (around 25 developers and over 50 adopters; a blog recap will be linked from the notes). It gives any community, geospatial or otherwise, a standard way to register how it stores metadata in Zarr: a conventions attribute carrying at least one of a UUID, spec URL, or schema URL, plus a template repository for creating new conventions. The early adopters are a GeoProj (CRS) convention and a Multiscales convention, both adaptations of existing GeoZarr material. Max stressed that the framework is compatible with and helpful to GeoZarr rather than separate: the developer-facing Markdown conventions would align completely with the OGC GeoZarr document, serving organizations that need the OGC stamp as well as developers who find OGC documents hard to parse. He suggested releasing GeoProj first (the smallest component and the most urgent request), then Multiscales, then common data model components, noting that Proj does not require a CDM to come first. The next most urgent convention after those is a tile matrix set convention, composable with Multiscales (Davis Bennett has demonstrated the composition), serving renderers that load Zarr directly and need TMS information, while intermediate APIs such as TiTiler or Flux do not.

Ryan asked the group to settle a procedural question: is GeoZarr, in practice, a collection of composable conventions, released one at a time and driven by the features people actually need? He advocated yes, since it defines concrete work: write each convention down and implement it in software. Christophe agreed, admitting the group had so far produced nothing usable, expressed interest in the multiscales work, and asked to pass the baton on the presentational aspects of the spec. Emmanuel reported the Multiscales convention had matured through a month of iteration and could be released as a candidate together with Proj, with the EOPF data model as the most viable validation candidate; the convention is deliberately domain-agnostic (bioimaging included) and composes with domain-specific conventions. Christophe cautioned from his own tests that viewers need geospatial-specific overview information (tile counts, tile matrix sets) beyond generic multiscales; Max distinguished the direct-renderer and intermediate-API use cases and positioned the TMS convention as the third key piece rather than a blocker.

**Decided:** proceed with Zarr conventions as the basis of GeoZarr V1. Max will continue the co-working sessions (Monday, Wednesday, and Friday at 9:30 AM Pacific, calendar invite to follow) and will release the GeoProj and Multiscales release candidates by Friday of the following week at the latest.

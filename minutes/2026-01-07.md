# GeoZarr SWG Monthly Meeting, 7 January 2026

## 1. Round-robin updates

- **Ryan (Zarr steering council):** a governance update is in progress to clarify the bounds of the NumFOCUS-sponsored project, so efforts like GeoZarr and the conventions can operate more autonomously; an announcement is expected within the month. On the conventions blog post Alistair started at the summit: publish once the framework is demonstrably in use, with GeoZarr as the worked example; Max is invited to co-author.
- **Emmanuel:** OpenLayers has an initial PR supporting Multiscales and the geospatial conventions, with working examples, already used for EOPF; EOPF Explorer V1 will be delivered to ESA at the end of the month with the Zarr conventions as its foundation; CarbonPlan's zarr-layer has an EOPF example using Multiscales and GeoProj rendering on the globe (bands cannot be combined yet); Even Rouault is working on the GDAL side; TiTiler is already compliant with Multiscales.
- **Max:** version 1 of the Zarr Conventions spec was released in December, with working versions of GeoProj and Spatial; PRs polishing the GeoZarr repo will merge on Friday; old Fathom notes will be compiled into Agora. The GeoZarr examples repo and website (built for version 0.4) need updating with real implementations; EOPF Explorer will keep a page listing software and services using the conventions, usable as a reference.
- **Wietze:** experimenting with the QGIS STAC plugin to load GeoZarr directly, previewing a STAC item and inspecting pixel values, built on the latest GDAL with some tweaks; shard support is a remaining limit. The goal is to drop a GeoZarr into QGIS the way a COG is. He will share the PR in the CNG Slack for feedback when ready. Chris recounted the EDR experience of funding a QGIS plugin (and having to re-fund it when QGIS moved on) and may investigate funding if this becomes important.

## 2. CF engagement

Chris Little: the big met services are moving infrastructure into the cloud, and Zarr is an important solution at their scale; the interest is a CF convention for the geo part, since important archives already use CF. His risks: the CF convention's academic authors do not always engage with practical realities, and the met and environmental communities are paying too little attention and contributing no resources (he is himself approaching retirement). He suggested approaching David Hassell and Jonathan Gregory at Reading University (Gregory being one of the original founders of the CF convention) and will sound out the UK Met Office for effort.

Ryan offered a counterweight: Zarr with xarray has been used for close to ten years, predominantly for met, ocean, and climate model data under CF conventions, with successful informal adoption ("we one-to-one copied the NetCDF metadata into Zarr and it worked"); this group exists because of the traditional GIS and remote-sensing world, where CRS and coordinate encoding blocked adoption. Formalizing the CF usage matters, but he sees no large risk to met data. Chris's concern runs the other direction as well: that Zarr drifts too far into a geospatial "everything is a feature or a coverage" worldview at the expense of the four-dimensional met perspective. Tyler observed that this GIS-versus-Earth-system-modeling tension is what has made the group move slowly, and that conventions layered on Zarr seem more palatable to both sides.

Max's approach for CF: mostly codify, via Zarr conventions, what people already do, so nobody has to abandon existing progress. Asked whether CF leadership would rather drive this, Chris expected a hard line ("there is a CF convention and they are not changing it"), noting there is a CF NetCDF convention but no CF Zarr convention, and that David Hassell chips into the repo occasionally but the posture is monitoring, not proposing.

**Decided:** Max will write an issue in the GeoZarr repository outlining how the roadmap lets those who need new features build them while codifying existing CF practice; Chris will circulate it for comment.

## 4. Versioning across conventions

Max presented the pillars figure (Proj, Spatial, and Multiscales as pillars of the GeoZarr specification, formalized through the OGC process) and opened an issue on consistent versioning across the conventions: integer versioning, full semantic versioning, or JSON-schema-driven. No decision was needed in the meeting; input was requested within about a week so guidance can go into GeoZarr.

## 5. Goals before February

The weekly Zarr conventions co-working sessions continue an hour before this slot each Wednesday (on the Zarr community calendar), focused on DGGS, which Justus Magin has been working on, and on enabling CF conventions registration. Emmanuel expects to release the EOPF Explorer visualization, the first key GeoZarr implementation for the examples repo. The named gap to success is updating the GDAL implementation, which was merged against the Zarr-Summit-era conventions and has drifted; Emmanuel's project is funding Even Rouault for GDAL read support of the conventions and onboarded him that week. Chris set two targets: find Max a co-chair (it cannot be a second Development Seed person, so it must be one further OGC member) and, against silent meetings, produce strawman documents to force feedback. Max committed to a strawman proposal for the February meeting: the pillars plus the CF component.

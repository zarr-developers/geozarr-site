## 2. Introductions and interests: Eugene Yu

Eugene attended for the second time, representing his supervisor, who knows Max personally. His organization is a NASA data provider (one of the original DAACs, in the transition toward Earthdata) whose holdings are largely NetCDF. Their interest is in moving data into the cloud in a cloud-optimized format that supports random access, tiled retrieval, and visualization directly, without an API service standing between the data and clients, to keep operational cloud costs down. A colleague of his works on virtual Zarr and Kerchunk approaches; Eugene himself focuses on actually converting data into the target format.

Max pointed him to two resources:

- The implementations page, a matrix of tool support for the three conventions that currently comprise GeoZarr. For example, GDAL has full read-write support and rioxarray was read-only as of the last update (write support may since have landed; the page can lag).
- The virtual Zarr co-working series organized by Max's Development Seed colleague Aimee Barciauskas, whose adoption into ESIP was kicking off the same day, with presentations on virtual-store architecture from PO.DAAC and on templates from Sean Harkins. Max noted this is relevant for providers who do not want to duplicate large NetCDF holdings as native Zarr.

Eugene will evaluate the listed tools and run internal demos. He also reported on GeoHEIF, the GEOINT community's geospatial profile of the High Efficiency Image Format (developed with GIMI, which covers both static and motion imagery), which he evaluated in OGC Testbeds 20 and 21 and considers easy to map to NetCDF. He particularly likes its compact CRS referencing: a short standardized identifier (e.g. an EPSG or ESRI code) whose full definition is retrieved externally, rather than a long inline string, which saves storage at scale. He hopes GeoZarr will support something similar; Max will review the GeoHEIF/GIMI documents.

## 3. Updates from Emmanuel

- **CNES cloudification study.** Emmanuel is conducting a study for CNES, the French space agency, on cloudification of their data formats. One deliverable is an addition to the cloud-native geospatial guide: a decision framework, essentially a decision tree with evaluation gates (some tied to format maturity) leading to a recommended cloud-native format, GeoZarr among them. It is open as a pull request, under review by the guide's maintainer, and the group is invited to comment.
- **Benchmarking framework.** The study also produced an open, CNES-funded benchmarking framework for quantified format comparisons. It takes a source format, a target cloud-native format, and a transformer, then applies benchmarks ranging from simple size and compression comparisons to a display benchmark that starts a TiTiler instance, mimics map browsing, and measures response times; Zarr can be tested with and without multiscale pyramids. It currently covers GeoTIFF, Zarr, GeoZarr, and some COPC, fed a report delivered to CNES, and Emmanuel hopes it becomes a shared framework the community contributes to.
- **CNG Forum.** Emmanuel has two talks at the Cloud-Native Geospatial Forum in Utah in October, one of them a 20-minute report on GeoZarr ("making GeoZarr a true geospatial API"). Input from the group is welcome.
- **EOPF renewal.** ESA has contracted another year of EOPF work, which will fund development around GeoZarr: improvements to Zarr-Python (Davis Bennett working on this) and an async GeoZarr reader for TiTiler.

## 4. Explicit coordinates: findings from the working sessions

Max hosted the working sessions on explicit coordinates at the beginning of August and will circulate a written summary. The findings:

- There was shared agreement that explicit 1D coordinates are the must-have. Most participants agreed that 2D coordinate arrays, cell bounds, non-standard calendars, band names / category labels / wavelengths, and coordinates shared within a group are necessary. A few wanted parametric vertical coordinates, though it was not a dominant use case, and only a couple highlighted DGGS as a near-term need.
- The one sharp disagreement was whether GeoZarr should recommend exactly one way to do each thing: a must-have for Deepak and Christophe, and something Patrick said he would fundamentally oppose. Max's proposed near-term path is to start with exactly one way, since that decision is reversible whereas multiple ways cannot later be consolidated; failing consensus, the group would have to vote.

## 5. Release strategy: 1.0 now, coordinates in 1.1

Christophe was the only working-session participant who felt strongly that explicit coordinates must be in version 1. Max sees this as in tension with the goal of releasing soon, and will follow up with him on releasing 1.0 with the current three conventions while developing coordinate support in parallel. Emmanuel supported this: the coordinates discussion should not postpone the release, the three pillars are a solid foundation, and since coordinates are additive rather than breaking they fit naturally as a 1.1, which is also easier for the community to understand.

## 6. Resolving the two coordinates proposals

Two proposals for explicit coordinates exist: Patrick's, which builds on CF and references existing OGC standards, and Christophe's, a composable framework in which the framework describes coordinates generically and each coordinate type is delegated to its own convention (DGGS to a DGGS convention, a future time convention, and so on). The choice between them has not been made, and Max was frank that he does not yet see a clean merge path, particularly given the one-way versus multiple-ways divide; a vote is the fallback.

Eugene observed that the source communities assign coordinates in genuinely different ways (imagery formats like GeoTIFF assume regular grids, the NetCDF world uses explicit coordinate arrays that also support irregular grids, and DGGS indexes cells rather than assigning coordinates), so full consensus on a single representation may be unrealistic. He suggested a simple base plus optional extensions, citing GeoJSON's fixed default CRS and the base-plus-parts design of WFS. Emmanuel replied that this is exactly what the composable proposal provides, and that reintegrating CF wholesale into GeoZarr is impossible given its size; instead, a CF convention relying on the actual CF conventions could plug into the framework. Those present were sympathetic to the composable approach, but no decision was taken in this small session.

On governance if consensus fails: Emmanuel asked what body decides, drawing on STAC's history (first a benevolent dictator, later a PSC). Max explained that in the OGC model the SWG itself plays that role through its voting members. Regular participants who are not OGC members, such as Jonathan and Patrick, are not on the voting roster unless they join; Eugene confirmed he has signed up through Agora. The Agora transition lost the SWG's voting roster, which Max is working to restore and communicate.

On meeting in person: a Zarr Summit would help but probably cannot happen before next spring, too late for this question. A smaller session in Europe with Christophe and Patrick, plus a third person to facilitate, could happen sooner.

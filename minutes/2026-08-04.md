# GeoZarr SWG Monthly Meeting, 4 August 2026

**Date:** Tuesday 4 August 2026, 9:00 AM US Eastern
**Attendees:** Max Jones (chair, Development Seed), Eugene Yu, Kathi Schleidt, Patrick Van Laake, Wietze Suijker, Emmanuel Mathot
**Subject of the meeting:** Review the revised GeoZarr SWG charter

## 1. Welcome and recap

Max opened with a recap for newer participants: GeoZarr is an OGC standard-in-progress for encoding geospatial data in Zarr, based on work originally started by Christophe Noël, independently under an ESA contract. In December 2025 the SWG pivoted from a monolithic from-scratch specification to a profile that selects and integrates small, composable Zarr conventions, and the roadmap agreed after the pivot set the V1 deliverable as three conventions: `proj`, `spatial`, and `multiscales`, each in its own repository under zarr-conventions. Concerns subsequently raised that the V1 deliverable excludes explicit coordinates, which are widely used, led to the present rechartering exercise: writing down explicitly what is in the SWG's scope and how the group aims to work.

**Correction to the record:** Patrick noted that the recap misattributed the coordinates objection to him; it was raised by Christophe. Patrick agrees with Christophe that the `spatial` convention has its niche and user base but is not comprehensive enough to serve all users of geospatial data, so data structures with explicit coordinates are needed, but he does not consider that they must be part of V1.

## 4. Revised charter

### 4.1 Review process and format

The charter is drafted in Markdown because it is easier to write and to comment on in GitHub; the HTML will be generated from AsciiDoc after agreement, with no substantive changes, so reviewers need only read the Markdown. Max proposed pausing the meeting for a short independent reading; Emmanuel objected that reviewing the draft was already the homework from the previous meeting and that postponing review each month stalls progress. The group instead went through the comments on the pull request directly.

Two general observations on charters were recorded: Kathi noted that a SWG is not held to delivering everything in its charter, but anything intended to become a document needs a clear hook in it; Emmanuel said the charter should settle how the SWG works, including how it evaluates and admits conventions and the conformance model, so that effort can move from process discussions to substance, and serve as essential orientation for anyone joining.

### 4.2 Intended Capabilities table

Patrick took the view that a charter should elaborate intent one level above tangible outputs (a statement such as "support as many users of geospatial data with their specific requirements as possible"), and that the Intended Capabilities table reads as outputs, whose detail belongs in a work-plan document. Max responded that the table is not a set of conventions but the scope the SWG intends to support: well-established capabilities of geospatial data that people have brought to GeoZarr over the past three years. A newcomer can check whether their data is in scope by reading the table, instead of opening a long GitHub discussion.

Kathi asked whether the table should mark which capabilities are planned for the current cycle. Max had removed such a column from an earlier draft based on feedback from Patrick and Chris Little, since it risked relitigating the stalled V1-coordinates question. Instead, Max suggested enumerating the capabilities targeted for V1 in the Initial Deliverables section, so the scope-of-work table does not define the solution; Patrick agreed this matched his own suggestion.

Kathi also noted the near one-to-one duplication between the table and the prior-art bullet list in section 3.1, and proposed moving the bullet-point details into a prior-art column of the table, clearly framed as breadcrumbs to the past. Max saw the risk of coupling GeoZarr to past delineations, to be mitigated by an explicit statement that referencing prior art does not mean GeoZarr will follow it exactly; the resulting table size might be managed with toggleable cells in the rendered HTML.

**Decided:** the table stays in the charter; the capabilities targeted for V1 will be enumerated under Initial Deliverables; section 3.1 will be reduced to prose and the prior-art details consolidated into a column of the table, with a statement that prior art is not binding.

### 4.3 Referenceable introductory prose

Kathi pointed out a standards-writing practice the OGC template omits: prose sitting between the "Scope of Work" heading and subsection 3.1 cannot be referenced by clause number, so it should live under its own subheading.

**Decided:** a "General" subheading will be added under Section 3 (Scope of Work). Max will also suggest this to the TC as template feedback.

### 4.4 Observational semantics (OMS)

Kathi asked for a hook for OMS semantics in the capabilities table, both as prior art and as functionality of interest. Her core concern is that gridded standards capture the spatial structure and deliver values, but not the methodology ("you get a number that is the soil moisture; if you're lucky, a unit of measure. How did you get those values?").

Patrick responded that OMS fits the general model like any other family of conventions: just as regular imagery maps to `spatial` + `proj` + `multiscales`, and CF will map to a set of conventions, OMS can be implemented as one or more conventions by the OMS experts, interfacing with people familiar with Zarr; it does not require specific design effort from this SWG. Speaking as a CF conventions author, he stressed that one size does not fit all: `spatial`, his CF-based CS convention, and an OMS convention would each serve particular user bases without conflicting, and the community will determine what is valuable. He offered to discuss it further with Kathi one-on-one.

Emmanuel agreed: GeoZarr's core function is data structure, and a format container does not carry the procedures that produced the data. The recharter frames GeoZarr as a profile precisely so that something like OMS becomes its own convention on its own track, assessed against the charter's incorporation criteria for a later version. The semantic alignment already exists upstream in Kathi's own work. He noted he implemented CoverageJSON over GeoZarr in TiTiler last month, already conveying far more of the data's meaning than COG can. He encouraged Kathi to develop the OMS convention respecting GeoZarr's principles (composability, domain-agnosticity) and then propose it to the SWG.

Kathi remained concerned about where the hook is and whether independently developed conventions end up orthogonal and never compose. Max synthesized: OMS defines metadata about how values are derived, and some of the capabilities it intends (units of measure, descriptive metadata, statistical provenance) are already in the table.

**Decided:** OMS will be added as related/prior art, and any OMS capabilities not already covered by the table will be enumerated in it. The OMS convention itself will be developed as a separate, composable convention rather than by modifying the core GeoZarr model.

### 4.5 "Standard name" wording

Prompted by Kathi asking where a GeoZarr package describes what the data actually is (soil moisture vs. soil organic carbon), Max observed that "standard name" in the capabilities wording is CF-derived jargon and should be restated as describing the observed property (e.g. air temperature at 2 m). Kathi added that CF standard names aggregate components (property, height, sometimes methodology) that OMS and I-ADOPT deliberately disaggregate.

**Decided:** the capability will be reworded to mean describing the observed property, without the CF jargon.
